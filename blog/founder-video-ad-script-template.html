How to Write a Founder Video Ad Script (With a Free Template)

Published 25 October 2026 · 6 min read

A founder talking to camera is one of the simplest ads a small brand can make. You need a phone, a window and something worth saying. The hard part is the something worth saying. Most founder ads fall flat for the same reason: the script was written to be read, not spoken. This guide shows you how to write a founder video ad script, with a free template you can fill in tonight, a worked example and the habits that make a script sound like you.

The short answer: a founder video ad works best at 30 to 60 seconds and in five parts. Open with a hook, name the problem, say why you made the product, show proof, then ask for one clear action. Draft it by talking out loud, not by typing, and cut anything you would not say to a customer in person.

Why founder ads are worth making

People buy from people. A founder can say things a polished brand ad cannot: why the product exists, what went wrong in early versions, what they refused to compromise on. That kind of detail is hard to fake, and viewers tend to trust it.

Founder ads are also cheap to test. One person, one phone and one afternoon can produce several versions, each opening with a different hook. That matters more than production value, because the hook usually decides whether anyone watches the rest.

The founder video ad script template, in five parts

The timings below are for a 45 second ad. Stretch or squeeze them for 30 or 60 seconds, but keep the order.

  1. Hook (0 to 5 seconds). One line that makes the right person stop scrolling. A blunt claim, a question they ask themselves, or something surprising about how the product is made.
  2. Problem (5 to 15 seconds). Describe the problem in the words your customers use. Lift phrases straight from reviews, emails and support chats.
  3. Why you made it (15 to 25 seconds). The short version of your story. What annoyed you enough to start, and what you did differently.
  4. Proof (25 to 38 seconds). Show it working. A demonstration, a real review read out, or a detail only someone who cares would know. Only use proof you can back up.
  5. Ask (38 to 45 seconds). One action. Tell them what to do and what happens next, such as "Tap the link and try the starter pack".

Copy this into a note and fill in the gaps:

  • Hook: "If you [have this problem], [surprising truth]."
  • Problem: "Most [products] [let you down in this way]. I know because [your experience]."
  • Why you made it: "So I spent [time] making one that [does the thing differently]."
  • Proof: "Here's what that looks like. [Show it.] [One customer line.]"
  • Ask: "If that sounds like you, [action]. [What they get.]"

A worked example

Here is the template filled in for a made up brand selling a hand cream for people who work with their hands. The brand and the review are invented for this example.

  • Hook: "If your hands crack every winter, the problem probably isn't the cold."
  • Problem: "Most hand creams sit on top of your skin. You wash once and it's gone. I'm a joiner, so I wash my hands about twenty times a day."
  • Why you made it: "So I spent two years with a small lab in Yorkshire making one that stays on through a full shift."
  • Proof: "Watch. Washed, dried, and it's still there. One customer told us it's the first winter her knuckles haven't bled."
  • Ask: "If that's you, tap the link and grab the trial tube. It's small enough for a work bag."

Read it out loud and it runs at roughly 40 seconds. Notice that nothing in it is clever. It is just specific, which is what makes it believable.

How to make it sound like you

Founders who sound stiff on camera usually sound fine in a voice note. So start there.

  • Talk first, type second. Record yourself answering "why did you make this?" as if a friend asked. Transcribe it and build the script from your own sentences.
  • Use short sentences. If you need a breath in the middle of a line, split it in two.
  • Keep your contractions. "It's" and "I'm", not "it is" and "I am". Written English sounds formal when spoken.
  • Cut brand words. If a phrase belongs on a label ("premium", "innovative", "game changing"), take it out.
  • Read it aloud twice. Anywhere you stumble, rewrite it the way you naturally said it.

Six hook starters

Write five or six hooks for every script, film them all, and let the results pick the winner.

  1. "Most people get [this] wrong, and it's not their fault."
  2. "I started [brand] because I was fed up with [problem]."
  3. "Here's what nobody tells you about [category]."
  4. "This is the [product] I wish I'd had [when]."
  5. "We nearly didn't launch this, because [honest reason]."
  6. "If you [do this every day], watch this."

Filming it on your phone

  • Face a window. Daylight from in front is kinder than any ring light you can buy in a hurry.
  • Hold the phone at eye level and look at the lens, not the screen.
  • Film vertical for Instagram, Facebook and TikTok placements.
  • Record in short chunks. Do each part of the template as its own take. It is easier to get right and easier to edit.
  • Add captions. Plenty of people watch with the sound off, so the words need to be on screen.

Common mistakes

  • Starting with your name. "Hi, I'm the founder of..." wastes the first three seconds. Lead with the hook and introduce yourself later, if at all.
  • Listing every feature. One problem and one benefit per ad. Save the rest for the next one.
  • Making claims you cannot prove. Ad platforms and the Advertising Standards Authority both expect you to back up what you say. Stick to what you can show.
  • Only filming one version. A single ad tells you very little. Several hooks on the same body tell you what people respond to.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a founder video ad be?

Usually 30 to 60 seconds. Shorter cuts of 15 to 20 seconds can work too, but they need a strong hook and a single point. Write the long version first, then cut it down.

Should I read from a script or speak freely?

Both. Learn the script well enough to say it in your own words, then film several takes. Reading word for word from a screen tends to show in your eyes. A short bullet list beside the lens is a good middle ground.

What if I hate being on camera?

Most founders do at first. Film the hook ten times and it gets easier. If it still feels wrong, the same template works for a voiceover over product footage, or for a customer or creator to read.
