SEO for Product Brands: 7 Fixes That Pay Off

Published 20 February 2026 · Updated 10 October 2026 · 5 min read

Plenty of product brand sites get visits from searches that never turn into orders, while the collection and product pages that should be found are thin, duplicated or hard to reach. More of the right visitors is worth far more than more visitors.

These are the seven fixes we start with on any online shop, Shopify or not. Most are a few hours of work, and you can check them all with free tools.

1. Write titles and headings the way people search

Your page title is the blue link Google shows in results. It should use the words a buyer types, not your internal product name. For a collection, “Linen Shirts for Men” will usually do better than “The Summer Edit”. For a product, include the product type and the detail that matters most, such as material, size or use.

Keep titles to roughly 60 characters so they are not cut off, and give every page its own. The H1 on the page should say the same thing in plain words, once.

To find the words, check the queries that already bring you impressions in the Performance report in Google Search Console. The suggestions Google shows as you type help too.

2. Give collection and product pages real content

Collection pages often rank for the most valuable searches, yet many are just a grid of products. Add a short introduction that says what is in the range and who it is for, and a few lines further down that answer common questions.

On product pages, write your own descriptions. Copying the manufacturer's text means your page says the same as every other shop selling it. Cover what it is, what it is made of, sizes, care, delivery and returns.

3. Show trust where people decide

Search brings people to the page. Whether they buy depends on what they find there. Reviews, clear delivery times, a visible returns policy and real photos all help. Reviews also add fresh words to the page in the language your customers use, which can help it match more searches.

If you make or ship from the UK, say so near the price. Buyers like to know where something comes from and how quickly it will arrive.

4. Link your pages together properly

Internal links tell Google which pages matter, and they help visitors move towards a purchase.

  • Link from your homepage and main menu to your most important collections.
  • Link from each blog post to the collection or product it relates to, using descriptive words such as “our linen shirts”, never “click here”.
  • Link related products to each other, such as a product and its refill.
  • Look for pages with no internal links pointing to them. Google may struggle to find them, and so will visitors.

5. Get the technical basics right

You do not need an expensive audit to catch the big problems. Check these:

  • One main address per page. On Shopify, a product opened from inside a collection can appear under a second URL. Make sure each one points to its main URL with a canonical tag.
  • A clean sitemap. Shopify creates one at /sitemap.xml. Submit it in Search Console and check that the pages it lists are ones you want found.
  • Nothing important hidden. No key pages marked noindex or blocked in robots.txt.
  • Fast pages on a phone. Compress large images and remove apps you no longer use, since many add scripts to every page.
  • Product structured data. This lets Google show price, stock and reviews in results. Most good Shopify themes include it, but check with Google's Rich Results Test.

6. Write content that leads to a product

Blog posts earn their place when they answer questions buyers ask before they buy: how to choose, how to use, how two options compare. “How to choose a running backpack” can lead straight to your backpacks. “The history of hiking” probably cannot.

Group posts around your main collections, so each one has a few helpful articles linking to it. That gives Google a clear picture of what the site is about.

7. Measure orders, not just visits

Traffic is easy to grow and easy to misread. Make sure Google Analytics records purchases, then look at organic search visits by landing page. Which pages bring visitors who buy? Pair that with Search Console to see which searches bring those visits.

A page with modest traffic and steady orders deserves more attention than a blog post with thousands of visits and no sales.

Frequently asked questions

How long does SEO take to work?

Small fixes, such as better titles, can show once Google crawls the page again. New pages and content usually take a few months to settle, depending on how established your site is and how competitive the searches are.

Is blog content enough on its own?

No. A blog helps most when it points to strong collection and product pages. If those pages are thin, fix them first.

Does Shopify take care of SEO for us?

It covers some basics, such as a sitemap, canonical tags and editable titles. It does not write your titles, collection text or product descriptions, and themes and apps can slow pages down. The platform gives you the tools. The work is still yours.
